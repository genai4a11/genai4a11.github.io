SECTION 01

What Is Sora?

Sora is OpenAI's video generation model announced in February 2024 and released to the public in December 2024. It can generate up to 60 seconds of photorealistic or stylized video from a text prompt, extend existing videos, and fill in missing frames. It represented a significant leap in temporal coherence, camera motion control, and multi-object interaction fidelity compared to prior video models.

SECTION 02

Architecture

Sora uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture. Video is encoded into spacetime patches — small latent chunks spanning both spatial pixels and temporal frames. A transformer denoises these patches jointly, allowing the model to reason about motion, physics, and scene consistency across the entire clip simultaneously. This contrasts with earlier frame-by-frame or RNN-based approaches that degraded over time. The model is conditioned on text via CLIP/T5-style embeddings.

SECTION 03

Prompt Engineering

Effective Sora prompts describe: subject, action, camera angle, lighting, and style. Overly short prompts produce generic results; specificity improves adherence. Example prompt patterns:

# Good Sora prompt structure
PROMPT = (
    'A golden retriever runs across a snow-covered meadow at sunset, '
    'slow motion, 24mm lens, soft warm light, cinematic depth of field'
)
# Style references work well
STYLE_PROMPT = (
    'An aerial shot of Tokyo at night, neon reflections on wet streets, '
    'in the style of Blade Runner, anamorphic lens flare'
)
SECTION 04

Capabilities & Limitations

Sora excels at camera motion, lighting variation, and short-clip realism. Limitations include: physics violations in complex interactions (liquid, cloth, hands), text rendering within video, and consistency across cuts when stitching multiple clips. Long-form narrative coherence (>30s) can drift. The model includes safety filters that decline prompts involving real people, violence, and other policy-violating content.

SECTION 05

API Access

Sora is available via the OpenAI API and as a feature in ChatGPT Pro. API access uses the video generation endpoint. Cost is per-second of generated video. Generated videos are watermarked with C2PA metadata for provenance tracking.

SECTION 06

Comparison to Alternatives

Competing video generation models include Runway Gen-3 Alpha, Kling (Kuaishou), Pika Labs, and Stable Video Diffusion. Sora leads in duration and cinematic quality; Runway Gen-3 is competitive on style control; Kling excels at realistic human motion. Open-source options (CogVideo, Open-Sora) lag in quality but allow local deployment without usage restrictions.
